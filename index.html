About Burnout Types: Maslach Model, Symptoms & FAQ

Burnout is recognized by the World Health Organization (ICD-11 QD85) as an occupational phenomenon resulting from chronic workplace stress. According to Christina Maslach's research, burnout manifests through emotional exhaustion, cynicism/depersonalization, and reduced professional efficacy.

Occupational Boundary: This reflection tool helps identify behavioral stress patterns. It is an educational prompt, not a clinical psychiatric diagnosis or medical assessment.

Six Authored Burnout Patterns

  • The Overachiever: Perfectionism, guilt during rest, and hyper-responsibility leading to physical exhaustion.
  • The Empath: Compassion fatigue from absorbing colleagues' emotional distress without personal boundaries.
  • Boredom / Under-Challenge: Boreout from monotonous tasks, lack of growth, and severe mental stagnation.
  • Invisible Burnout: High-functioning exterior masking internal emotional collapse and solitary struggle.
  • Creative Burnout: Inability to produce novel ideas due to cognitive depletion and constant client demands.
  • Digital Burnout: Sensory overload from notifications, constant connectivity, and screen fatigue.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How does the Maslach Burnout framework define burnout?

A: Maslach identifies three core dimensions: emotional exhaustion (feeling drained), depersonalization (cynical detachment), and reduced personal efficacy (feeling ineffective).

Q: What is the difference between stress and burnout?

A: Stress involves too much pressure and over-engagement; burnout is characterized by depletion, emptiness, emotional numbness, and disengagement.

Q: Is this self-assessment a medical diagnosis?

A: No. It is an educational tool reflecting work-stress patterns, not a clinical diagnosis. Consult medical professionals for clinical evaluation.

Q: Are my responses and results stored or tracked?

A: Never. All calculations are executed locally in your browser. No workplace choices, answers, or burnout scores are collected or stored.
